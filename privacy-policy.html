Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 November 2026

Firebreak checks a web link before it opens. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not send anywhere. It describes the app's real behaviour; where the app has two modes, both are described, because which one applies depends on the build you installed.

The short version

  • There is no account and no sign-up. We never ask who you are.
  • There is no advertising ID, no analytics SDK and no third-party tracker.
  • The link checks that run on your phone send nothing anywhere at all.
  • We never sell or share your data, because we do not hold data about you.

1. What Firebreak does on your phone

When you tap a link, Firebreak examines the web address on the device itself. It compares that address against a list of well-known organisations and popular sites built into the app, looking for imitations — an address that copies a real name but changes the ending, or one that uses lookalike letters from another alphabet.

These checks need no internet connection. They work in airplane mode. Nothing about them leaves your phone.

Firebreak also keeps two running totals on the device — how many links it has checked and how many downloads it has blocked — along with your settings. This is stored only in the app's private storage on your phone, is never transmitted, and is erased when you uninstall the app.

2. When Firebreak contacts a server

Some builds of Firebreak can also open a page in a sandbox on our server, to photograph it and read its text without your phone ever loading it. This only happens if a scanning server is configured in the app. If it is not, Firebreak makes no network request of its own and this entire section does not apply to you.

When a deep scan does run, the app sends over an encrypted HTTPS connection:

What is sentWhy
The web address you are checking It is the thing being checked. There is no way to check a link without it.
A random install identifier To count your free monthly scans.

About that identifier: it is a random value created when you install the app. It is not your phone's hardware ID, advertising ID, phone number, or any account name, and it cannot be traced back to you or your device by us or by anyone else. The server stores only a one-way cryptographic hash of it, never the value itself. Reinstalling the app produces a new, unrelated identifier.

We do not send your contacts, your messages, your SMS inbox, your location, your installed apps, or anything else from your phone. Firebreak never reads your messages: when you tap a link in a messaging app, Android hands Firebreak that one link and nothing else.

Retention. A scan result is cached briefly so re-checking the same link is fast, then discarded. Scan counts are kept for the current calendar month so the free allowance can reset.

3. Permissions

Firebreak requests only two, and neither is a sensitive permission that Android asks you to approve:

  • Internet access — used only for deep scans and subscription checks.
  • Network state — to tell whether you are online before trying.

Firebreak asks for no access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts, SMS, call logs, photos or files.

Firebreak will ask to be your default browser. This is how Android lets it see a link before another app opens it. It does not grant Firebreak access to your browsing history in any other browser.

4. Subscriptions

If you subscribe, the purchase is handled entirely by Google Play. We never see your card, your UPI ID or your billing address.

To confirm a subscription is genuine, the app sends the purchase token Google issued to our server, which checks it with Google's Play Developer API and records that this install is entitled to Pro. Google's own handling of your payment is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

5. Naming of other organisations

Firebreak names organisations such as banks and government services when it detects an address imitating them. This is what makes the warning useful. It does not mean Firebreak is affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to any of them.

6. Children

Firebreak is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. Since the app collects no personal information from anyone, it holds none about children either.

7. Your rights

Because Firebreak holds no information that identifies you, there is no account to access, export or delete.

To remove everything the app has stored on your phone, uninstall it, or use Android Settings → Apps → Firebreak → Storage → Clear data. To end a subscription, use Google Play → Payments & subscriptions.

8. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected, the updated version will be published here with a new date before the change takes effect.
